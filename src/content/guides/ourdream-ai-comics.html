What Is OurDream AI Comics?

OurDream AI Comics is a feature inside OurDream.ai that lets you create full multi-page comics and webtoons with AI. You can use your own characters or pick characters created by other users, then build your comic page by page using the built-in panel editor.

You don’t need to know how to draw or design a comic yourself. You basically choose your characters, pick a layout, explain what should happen in each panel, and let the AI generate the page for you.

One important thing to know: you currently need a desktop screen to create comics. The editor does not work on mobile yet, although finished comics can still be read on any screen.

How OurDream AI Comics Works

Each comic is made up of different types of pages.

You can create a cover, generate normal comic pages with multiple panels, and organize all your pages in the order you want. OurDream AI also shows a Text Page option, but at the time of writing it is still marked as coming soon.

You can also give your comic a name, description, and tags. These are shown when your comic appears in the public Comics listing. If you want to change the order of your pages later, you can simply use the Organize button.

Comic Pages vs Cover Pages

A Comic Page is a normal AI-generated page. You choose how many panels you want and which layout to use, then tell the AI what should happen in each panel.

A Cover Page is usually used for the front cover of your comic. It has separate fields for the title, subtitle, and “Written by” text, which are added directly to the generated image.

But Cover Pages are not only for covers. You can also use them whenever you want one big full-page image, such as a dramatic scene, a chapter opening, or a “Coming Next” page.

What Can You Generate With OurDream AI Comics?

OurDream AI Comics can be used for normal stories, realistic comics, anime-style stories, and adult comics. But let’s be real: a lot of people are probably going to use this feature for erotic comics and hentai.

Think about your favorite manga or hentai, except now you control the story. You could start with two adults meeting at a bar, have them go on a date, take the story back to an apartment, build a relationship, and eventually turn it into an explicit sex scene. You decide what happens next, what the characters wear, what they say, and how the story develops.

Can You Generate NSFW Comics?

Yes. You can generate NSFW comics with OurDream AI.

Nudity, erotic stories, and consensual adult sex scenes are supported. The Comic Studio can sometimes be more sensitive than OurDream AI’s normal image generator, though, so very explicit scenes may take a few attempts to generate properly.

The important word here is adult. Make sure your characters clearly look and are described as adults.

What OurDream AI Does Not Allow

OurDream AI is uncensored, but that does not mean literally anything goes.

According to its current moderation rules, you cannot generate things like:

  • ### Sexual content involving anyone under 18 or who appears under 18
  • ### Sexual violence or other non-consensual sexual content
  • ### Bestiality
  • ### Extreme or gratuitous violence
  • ### Content promoting serious crime
  • ### Terrorist content
  • ### Harmful drug-related content
  • ### Harassment or non-consensual impersonation of real people

Basically, adult fantasy is fine. Illegal, exploitative, or seriously harmful stuff is not.

How OurDream AI Comics Moderation Works

OurDream AI uses both automatic filters and human moderation.

The automatic filter checks your prompts while you generate. If it thinks your prompt breaks the rules, it can block the generation.

Public comics can also be reviewed by real moderators. So just because the AI lets you generate something does not automatically mean it will be approved for the public comic gallery.

Their Community Guidelines also apply to private content, so keeping something private does not mean the rules disappear.

Why Your Comic Prompt Gets Blocked

This part can be a little annoying.

During beta testing, we found that the automatic filter sometimes reacts to individual words instead of understanding the full story.

For example, a completely normal fantasy battle could get blocked because you used a word like “killed” or certain weapon terms.

So if your harmless prompt gets blocked, don’t immediately throw the whole story away. Read it again and see if one specific word could be triggering the filter.

That said, don’t use this as a way to sneak genuinely prohibited content through the moderation system. If the actual idea breaks OurDream AI’s rules, changing a few words does not suddenly make it allowed.

Can You Use Copyrighted Characters?

This is one area where I don’t want to give you a bullshit answer.

The Content Moderation Policy and Community Guidelines I checked do not clearly explain whether you can create comics using copyrighted characters like Scooby-Doo, Marvel characters, or characters from existing anime.

So I would not assume it is allowed just because the generator accepts the prompt.

I’m going to confirm this separately with OurDream AI before giving a definite answer.

How to Make a Comic With OurDream AI

Making a comic with OurDream AI is pretty simple once you understand how the editor works. The first time I opened it, there were definitely more options than I expected, but the basic process is: pick your characters, create a cover, choose your panels, describe what happens, and generate the page.

Here is the full process.

Step 1 — Open the Comic Generator

First, open the OurDream AI Comic Studio.

One important thing: you need to use a desktop or laptop. You currently cannot create comics on mobile. You can read finished comics on your phone, but the actual Comic Studio editor requires a bigger screen.

Step 2 — Create a New Comic

Create a new comic and start setting up your story.

You don't need to have the entire comic planned before you start. You can keep adding, editing, and moving pages around later.

Your comic also gets its own name, description, and tags. These are used when your comic appears on the public Comics page.

Step 3 — Add Your Characters

Next, choose the characters that will appear in your story.

You can add a maximum of three characters to one comic. These can be your own OurDream AI characters or community characters.

Adding a character costs 20 DreamCoins because OurDream AI creates something called a Model Sheet for that character.

If you only need a random background character for one scene, you can also use a character image as a reference instead of creating a full Model Sheet.

Step 4 — Generate Your Character Model Sheets

This part is important.

A Model Sheet is basically the reference OurDream AI uses to remember what your character looks like across different comic pages.

The Model Sheet is created from the character's profile image. If your character looks wrong on the Model Sheet, I recommend fixing this before you start generating your comic. Otherwise, you could end up fixing the same problem on every page.

You can regenerate a Model Sheet for 20 DreamCoins, and every version you generate is saved so you can switch back to an older version later.

Also, if you use the same character in another comic later, you don't need to pay for another Model Sheet. The existing one can be reused.

Step 5 — Create Your Cover

Now you can create the cover of your comic.

The Cover Page has three text fields:

  • Title — maximum 30 characters
  • Subtitle — maximum 45 characters
  • Written by — maximum 40 characters

OurDream AI adds these directly to the generated cover image. You can also leave them blank if you would rather create the text another way.

You can also use a Cover Page later in your comic when you want one big full-page image instead of multiple panels. For example, you could use one for a dramatic reveal or the start of a new chapter.

Step 6 — Choose Your Comic Layout

For normal comic pages, you first choose how many panels you want.

OurDream AI currently has layouts for 2, 3, 4, and 5 panels.

There are quite a few options, but you don't need to overthink it.

For example:

  • Rows work well for normal conversations.
  • Diagonal panels feel more active and work well for action or arguments.
  • Splash Top works well when one big moment is followed by smaller reactions.
  • Tall Left works well when two characters are confronting each other.
  • Stagger works well when a scene keeps getting more intense.

You can change the layout from page to page, so your entire comic doesn't need to use the same one.

Step 7 — Write Your Page Prompt

This is where you tell OurDream AI what should happen on the page.

The most important tip is to describe every panel by number.

Don't write:

Top panel: The woman enters the bar.

Instead write:

Panel 01: The woman enters the bar.

Then continue with Panel 02, Panel 03, and so on.

OurDream AI understands panel numbers much better than descriptions like "top left" or "bottom right."

I also recommend keeping the entire page prompt under around 200 words. Longer prompts make it more likely that the AI starts ignoring parts of your instructions.

Be specific about what you actually want to see. Describe the character, pose, outfit, camera angle, lighting, and important background details instead of just saying something like "romantic room."

I'll go much deeper into prompting later in this guide because this is probably the biggest difference between getting a good comic and getting complete AI nonsense.

Step 8 — Generate Your Comic Page

Once your prompt and layout are ready, generate the page.

Generating one comic page currently costs 20 DreamCoins. Regenerating the entire page also costs another 20 DreamCoins.

Don't panic if it takes longer than a normal OurDream AI image. According to the beta testing guide, comic pages and Model Sheets normally take longer to generate.

Once the page appears, check every panel before moving on.

Look at things like:

  • Is the correct character in each panel?
  • Are their clothes correct?
  • Did their pose suddenly change?
  • Does the background make sense?
  • Are there any weird AI mistakes?

Fixing these problems now is much easier than noticing them five pages later.

Step 9 — Add Dialogue and Speech Bubbles

I recommend generating the artwork without dialogue first and adding your speech bubbles afterward.

OurDream AI has a separate speech balloon editor where you can drag bubbles onto the finished page and type exactly what you want your characters to say.

This works much better than asking the image generator to create text because AI-generated dialogue can get shortened, changed, or completely messed up.

You can add normal speech bubbles, thought bubbles, captions, shout bubbles, and text boxes.

Adding and saving speech balloons currently costs 0 DreamCoins.

One thing to remember: each speech bubble has a maximum of 150 characters. Keep the dialogue short and natural instead of trying to squeeze an entire paragraph into one bubble.

Step 10 — Edit Any Mistakes

You will probably need to fix something. That's normal with AI comics.

OurDream AI gives you two main editing tools: the Brush Editor and Layers.

The Brush Editor is useful when one specific part of the image is wrong. You select the area and tell the AI what you want there instead.

For example, if the background is wrong, tell it what background you want rather than simply saying "remove the background."

The testing guide found that edits work much better when you tell the AI what to create, rather than what to delete.

Layers give you more control. They can separate things like characters, backgrounds, furniture, and objects so you can move, resize, hide, or edit them individually. You can even generate completely new objects as new layers.

Don't go crazy editing the same image, though. The guide found that after around three edits, the results start becoming less reliable.

Step 11 — Add More Pages

Once you're happy with the first page, create the next one and continue your story.

OurDream AI automatically uses the previous page as a reference. This helps keep the same characters, location, and overall look between pages.

This is useful when the story continues in the same room.

But if your characters suddenly move from a bedroom to a beach, remove the previous-page reference. Otherwise, parts of the bedroom can start appearing in your beach scene.

The same applies when changing outfits, locations, time of day, or making a major scene change.

Step 12 — Organize and Publish Your Comic

Once your comic is finished, go through every page one final time.

You can use the Organize button to change the page order at any time.

Before publishing, I recommend checking:

  • Your cover looks good.
  • Your title is readable.
  • Your pages are in the correct order.
  • Characters look consistent.
  • Speech bubbles contain the correct text.
  • Your comic name and description are filled in.
  • You added relevant tags.
  • You selected the correct Model Sheet versions.

The beta testing guide recommends checking all of these things before sending your comic for public review.

And that's basically it. You now have a full AI-generated comic without drawing a single panel yourself.

How Model Sheets Work

Model Sheets are one of the most important parts of OurDream AI Comics because they help the AI remember what your character looks like.

When you add a character to your comic, OurDream AI creates a Model Sheet based on that character’s current profile image. The comic generator then uses that sheet as a reference when generating new pages and videos.

Think of it like a character cheat sheet for the AI.

If your Model Sheet looks wrong, your comic will probably look wrong too. So I recommend checking it carefully before you start generating a bunch of pages.

Generating a new Model Sheet costs 20 DreamCoins. It can also take longer than a normal image generation, so don’t worry if it feels slow.

How to Keep Characters Consistent

The Model Sheet helps a lot, but it does not magically make every panel perfect.

The comic generator can still change things like:

  • Outfit
  • Pose
  • Accessories
  • Small visual details
  • Background details

The biggest mistake is assuming the AI will remember everything from the previous panel.

It often won’t.

If your character is sitting on a couch in Panel 01 and you want her to still be sitting in Panel 02, say that again.

If she is wearing a black dress, say she is still wearing the black dress.

The guide found that poses and outfits can drift even between panels on the same page.

For longer scenes, you can also use something like:

POSE LOCK: Sarah stays sitting on the couch unless a panel specifically says she moves.

This gives the AI a much clearer instruction to follow.

How to Change a Character’s Appearance

There are a few ways to change how your character looks.

The most direct way is to change the character’s profile image and generate a new Model Sheet.

The Model Sheet is based mainly on the profile image, so if your character looks completely different from what you want, I would fix the profile image first.

You can also change the appearance for just one comic page without changing the Model Sheet.

For example, you can write:

Sarah is wearing a red evening dress.

This tells the comic generator to use that outfit for this generation only. Your original Model Sheet stays the same.

This is useful when your character changes clothes during the story.

Maybe she starts the comic in normal clothes, changes into a dress for a date, and later changes again. You don’t need to create a completely new character every time.

How to Create Different Model Sheet Versions

Every time you generate another Model Sheet, OurDream AI saves the version.

You can then switch between old and new versions whenever you want.

This is actually pretty useful.

For example, you could have:

  • A normal version
  • A different outfit
  • A fantasy version
  • A version with extra gear or accessories

The beta guide even recommends creating different states for characters when important gear is missing from the original Model Sheet.

One thing to remember is that you currently cannot delete a generated Model Sheet version, so don’t generate 20 random versions just for fun.

How to Use the Same Character in Multiple Comics

This part can save you some DreamCoins.

If you already generated a Model Sheet for a character, you can use that same character in another comic without paying to generate the exact same Model Sheet again.

The existing sheet can be reused across future comics.

So if you are creating a series with the same main character, you don’t have to start from zero every time.

OurDream AI Comic Layouts

OurDream AI currently gives you different page layouts depending on how many panels you want.

You can choose between 2, 3, 4, or 5 panels.

Different layouts work better for different types of scenes, so I wouldn’t just use the same layout on every page.

2-Panel Layouts

For two-panel pages, you can choose:

  • Diagonal — two panels divided with a diagonal line
  • Rows — two horizontal panels
  • Tall Bottom — a smaller panel on top and a larger panel underneath
  • Wedge — two panels separated by a wedge-style cut

Two panels work well when you want to show one simple action followed by a reaction.

For example:

Panel 01: She opens the hotel room door.

Panel 02: She sees who is waiting inside.

Simple and easy to follow.

3-Panel Layouts

For three panels, you get:

  • Diagonal
  • Rows
  • Tall Left
  • Splash Top

I think three panels are probably the easiest to work with for normal storytelling.

Rows are good for conversations or slower scenes.

Diagonal works better when something dramatic or fast is happening.

Tall Left gives you one large panel and two smaller panels, which works well for confrontations.

Splash Top gives you one big image at the top followed by two smaller reactions underneath.

4-Panel Layouts

For four panels, you can choose:

  • Diagonal
  • Grid
  • Stagger
  • Strip Top

The Grid is your classic 2×2 comic layout.

The Stagger layout is better when the scene gets more intense step by step.

For example:

Panel 01: Two characters are talking.

Panel 02: One gets annoyed.

Panel 03: They start arguing.

Panel 04: Someone storms out.

That kind of scene works really well with four panels.

5-Panel Layouts

For five panels, you currently get:

  • Centre Wide
  • Diagonal
  • Splash Top
  • Two Columns

Five panels let you fit more story onto one page, but I wouldn’t use them all the time.

The more panels you add, the more things the AI has to get right at once.

For simple conversations, three panels are usually enough.

For something with a lot of small actions or reactions, five panels can work better.

How to Make a Full-Page Comic Panel

If you want one huge image instead of several smaller panels, use a Cover Page.

You are not limited to using Cover Pages for the actual cover of the comic.

You can also use one in the middle of the story for:

  • A dramatic reveal
  • A big sex scene
  • A new chapter
  • A location reveal
  • A “Coming Next” teaser

This gives you one full-page image without panel borders.

Which Layout Should You Use?

There is no single best layout.

It depends on what is happening in the scene.

The beta guide recommends:

  • Calm intro or setup: 3-panel Rows
  • Dramatic entrance: 3-panel Diagonal
  • Romantic moment with a surprise: 3-panel Splash Top
  • Rising tension: 4-panel Stagger
  • Face-to-face confrontation: 3-panel Tall Left
  • Emotional ending: 3-panel Rows
  • Big splash or teaser: Full-page Cover Page

Personally, I would start with three panels if you are making your first comic.

It gives you enough space to tell a story without asking the AI to handle too much at once.

How to Prompt OurDream AI Comics

Prompting is probably the most important part of making a good comic.

You can have a great character and a good story, but if your prompt is messy, the AI will start making its own decisions. Characters suddenly stand up, outfits change, rooms look different, and random stuff appears in the background.

The good news is that you don’t need to write some crazy complicated AI prompt. In my experience, simple and clear works better.

Write Prompts by Panel Number

Always tell OurDream AI what should happen in each panel by using the panel number.

For example:

Panel 01: Sarah sits at the bar holding a cocktail.

Panel 02: A man sits down next to her and starts talking to her.

Panel 03: Sarah smiles at him and leans closer.

Don’t write things like:

Top panel: Sarah sits at the bar.

Bottom-right panel: The man talks to her.

The Comic Studio understands Panel 01, Panel 02, Panel 03 much better. The layout you selected already decides where those panels appear on the page.

I would use this format for basically every page you create.

Keep Your Prompts Short

More detail is not always better.

The beta testing guide recommends keeping the full page prompt under roughly 200 words. Once prompts get too long, the AI can start ignoring parts of them or giving the wrong details more importance.

So don’t write an entire novel for one comic page.

Instead of this:

Panel 01: Sarah is sitting in this beautiful romantic bar with an amazing atmosphere and lots of people having fun while she feels excited but slightly nervous because this is the first time she has ever gone to this particular bar...

Keep it simple:

Panel 01: Sarah sits alone at a dark wooden bar, holding a cocktail. Warm amber lights behind her. Medium shot.

That is much easier for the AI to understand.

Describe Exactly What You Want to See

Try to describe what should actually be visible in the image.

Don’t just say:

A romantic bedroom.

What does that mean?

Instead, say something like:

Small hotel bedroom, white bed sheets, warm bedside lamps, dark wooden furniture, soft amber light.

The guide found that specific details work better than vague words like “beautiful,” “cozy,” or “modern.”

For each panel, I normally think about five things:

  • ### Who is in the panel?
  • ### What are they doing?
  • ### Where are they?
  • ### What does the camera show?
  • ### What important detail needs to stay consistent?

That is usually enough.

For example:

Panel 02: Sarah is sitting on the hotel bed wearing her black dress. The man stands near the door looking at her. Medium-wide shot. Warm light from the bedside lamp.

Simple, but the AI knows exactly what I want.

How to Use Negative Prompts

There is no separate negative prompt box inside Comic Studio.

If you don’t want something in the image, add a short instruction directly to your normal prompt.

For example:

No text, no speech bubbles.

That one is especially useful because the AI can sometimes create random speech bubbles even when you never asked for them.

Keep negative prompts short.

The guide recommends using only one or two negative instructions. A giant list like:

No text, no speech bubbles, no weird hands, no extra people, no bad anatomy, no extra fingers, no...

can actually make the results worse.

Tell the AI what you do want instead of spending half the prompt explaining what you don't want.

How to Use Character References

You can reference your added characters directly with @ followed by the character.

This helps the generator understand exactly which character you are talking about and what that character should be doing.

You can also manually add reference images.

For example, you could use a reference image for:

  • ### A specific location
  • ### A character who does not have a Model Sheet
  • ### A certain outfit
  • ### A visual style you want to copy

The guide says you can use the automatic previous-page reference plus one additional manual reference.

This is really useful if your comic takes place somewhere specific.

Instead of trying to describe the same apartment perfectly on every page, you can use an image of that apartment as a reference.

How Page References Work

OurDream AI automatically uses the previous comic page as a reference when you generate the next page.

Most of the time, this is great.

If Page 1 takes place in a bedroom and Page 2 continues the same scene, the previous page helps OurDream AI keep the room, characters, and general look similar.

But there are times when you should remove it.

For example:

Page 1: Sarah and the man are inside a bar.

Page 2: They leave.

Page 3: They are now inside his apartment.

For Page 3, I would remove the bar reference. Otherwise, OurDream AI might start putting bar stools or other parts of the previous scene inside the apartment.

The guide recommends removing the previous-page reference when you change:

  • ### Location
  • ### Time of day
  • ### Outfit
  • ### Scene
  • ### Overall visual style

You should also remove the Cover Page reference before generating your first normal comic page, because the title text from the cover can sometimes bleed into the next generation.

How to Keep the Same Background Between Pages

This is one of those things I learned is better to repeat than to trust the AI to remember.

If your story stays in the same room, keep describing the important parts of that room.

For example, imagine the characters are inside an apartment hallway.

Don’t write:

Panel 03: Sarah opens the door.

The AI might suddenly decide that the door opens onto a street.

Instead write:

Panel 03: Sarah opens the apartment door. Behind her is the same indoor hallway with carpeted floors, wooden apartment doors, and warm wall lights.

The guide found that the model does not reliably remember backgrounds that are outside the current panel.

You don’t need to describe the whole room again every single time.

Just repeat the details that matter.

How to Keep Poses and Outfits Consistent

This is probably one of the biggest things to watch.

OurDream AI can treat each panel almost like a separate image.

So imagine:

Panel 01: Sarah is sitting on the bed.

Panel 02: Sarah talks to the man.

If you don't say she is still sitting, the AI might suddenly generate her standing.

The same thing happens with clothes.

If she takes off her jacket in Panel 01, don't assume the AI remembers that in Panel 02\.

Say:

Sarah is still sitting on the bed, wearing her black dress without the jacket.

The guide recommends repeating the current pose and outfit in every panel where it matters.

It feels a little repetitive when you write the prompt, but it saves you from regenerating pages later.

How to Change a Character’s Outfit

You do not need to create a new Model Sheet every time your character changes clothes.

You can simply tell the AI what the character is wearing inside the page prompt.

For example:

Panel 01: Sarah is wearing a tight red evening dress and sits at the restaurant table.

That outfit only applies to that generation. It does not permanently change the character's Model Sheet.

This is perfect for stories where characters change clothes.

For example:

Page 1: Casual clothes.

Page 3: Evening dress.

Page 6: Lingerie.

Page 8: Different outfit again.

Just make sure you keep repeating the new outfit in the following panels until the character actually changes again.

How to Use POSE LOCK and OUTFIT LOCK

If you have a longer scene, you can make things even clearer by putting a short lock instruction at the top of your page prompt.

For example:

POSE LOCK: Sarah stays sitting on the bed. James stays standing next to the window unless a panel specifically says otherwise.

The guide recommends this for scenes that continue across several panels or pages.

You can do the same thing with clothing:

OUTFIT LOCK: Sarah wears the black dress in every panel on this page. She is not wearing her jacket.

This is especially useful when an old piece of clothing still appears somewhere in the scene.

For example, if Sarah took her jacket off and threw it over a chair, the AI might see the word “jacket” in your prompt and put it back on her.

So be painfully clear:

Sarah is wearing the black dress without the jacket. The jacket is lying on the chair in the background.

It sounds repetitive, but that is exactly what helps keep the comic consistent.

How to Add Text and Speech Bubbles

Once your comic page looks good, you can add the dialogue.

OurDream AI has a separate speech bubble tool, which is way better than asking the image generator to create the text inside the image.

You simply drag a speech bubble onto the page, point it toward the character who is talking, and type the text yourself. You can then move it, resize it, rotate it, and change things like the text size and border thickness.

The best part is that adding speech bubbles currently costs 0 DreamCoins.

Types of Speech Bubbles

There are six different types you can use:

  • ### Speech — normal character dialogue
  • ### Thought — for thoughts
  • ### Caption — useful for narration or things like “Three hours later...”
  • ### Shout — spiky bubble for yelling
  • ### Black Box — good for darker narration or inner thoughts
  • ### White Box — simple text box without a normal speech bubble

You definitely don't need to use all of them.

For most comics, normal Speech, Thought, and Caption bubbles will probably cover 90% of what you need.

Why You Should Add Dialogue After Generating

I strongly recommend generating your comic page without dialogue first.

Then add the dialogue afterward with the speech bubble tool.

If you ask the image generator to create a long sentence inside the actual image, it can shorten it, rewrite it, misspell words, or just completely mess it up.

The guide found that shorter dialogue works better, but using the separate speech bubble tool avoids most of this problem because you type the final text yourself.

So instead of prompting:

Panel 01: Sarah sits at the bar. Dialogue: "I've been waiting here for almost thirty minutes and I thought you weren't going to come."

Just generate:

Panel 01: Sarah sits at the bar looking slightly annoyed.

Then add the exact dialogue afterward.

Much easier.

Speech Bubble Character Limits

Each speech bubble has a maximum of 150 characters, including spaces.

That is actually a good thing because giant walls of text usually look terrible inside comics anyway.

If your character has a really long line, shorten it.

Instead of:

"I can't believe you've been sitting here this entire time waiting for me and you never even sent me another message."

You could use:

"You waited here this whole time?"

Then put the next part in another panel or another speech bubble if you really need it.

The guide recommends avoiding a third bubble for the same piece of dialogue because the page starts getting cluttered.

How to Stop Random AI Speech Bubbles

Here is a weird problem I would never have expected until actually using AI image generators.

Even if you don't put any dialogue in your prompt, the AI can sometimes randomly generate speech bubbles anyway.

Luckily, there is a simple fix.

Add this at the end of your page prompt:

No text, no speech bubbles.

That's it.

The beta guide found that this worked better than throwing a giant list of negative instructions at the AI.

Don't write:

No dialogue, no bubbles, no text, no letters, no words, no captions, no signs...

Keep it simple.

How Speech Bubble Layering Works

One thing you need to know is that speech bubbles are stacked in the order you add them.

Every new bubble goes on top of the previous one.

There is currently no button to change that order afterward.

So if two bubbles need to overlap, think about which one should be behind.

Add that one first.

Then add the bubble that should appear on top.

It is a tiny thing, but it can save you from having to redo the bubbles later.

How to Edit OurDream AI Comics

Your first generation will not always be perfect.

Maybe the AI gives your character the wrong hair, puts a random object in the room, changes the background, or gives someone six fingers because apparently AI still occasionally believes humans evolved differently.

You don't always need to regenerate the entire page.

OurDream AI gives you two main tools for fixing things: the Brush Editor and Layers.

How to Use the Brush Editor

The Brush Editor is useful when one specific part of the page is wrong.

You select the area you want to change and then describe what you want to appear there.

The important part is how you write the edit prompt.

Don't focus on what you want removed.

Tell the AI what should replace it.

For example, instead of:

Remove the weird object on the table.

Try:

Wooden table with a glass of red wine on top.

Or instead of:

Delete the speech bubble.

Tell it what should actually be behind the bubble:

Warm bedroom wall with soft amber lighting.

The AI is much better at creating something than understanding a command like "erase this."

How to Fix Characters and Images

You can use the Brush Editor for a lot more than tiny mistakes.

You can fix things like:

  • ### Wrong character
  • ### Wrong outfit
  • ### Hair color
  • ### Background
  • ### Objects
  • ### Text
  • ### Speech bubbles
  • ### Small visual mistakes

For example, if the AI generates the wrong woman in a panel, describe the correct character clearly.

You could write:

Sarah, long red hair, wearing a black evening dress, sitting at the bar holding a cocktail.

The more important the change is, the clearer I would make the prompt.

The guide also recommends mentioning things like clothing and hair color when replacing characters so the AI doesn't confuse them again.

How to Edit Multiple Panels at Once

You can also edit several panels in one go.

OurDream AI supports something called the BREAK technique.

It looks like this:

Panel 01: Change her dress to black.

BREAK

Panel 02: Add a glass of wine to the table.

BREAK

Panel 03: Change his shirt to white.

The word BREAK tells the editor that each instruction applies to a different part of the page.

You can even use BREAK several times inside the same panel if you need to make several separate changes.

I wouldn't go crazy with this, though. If you are trying to fix half the page at once, it may be easier to regenerate it.

How to Use Layers

Layers give you much more control than the Brush Editor.

You first generate your comic page normally.

Then you click Create Layers.

OurDream AI analyzes the page and tries to separate important parts of the image into individual layers.

For example:

  • ### Character
  • ### Background
  • ### Table
  • ### Chair
  • ### Weapon
  • ### Other objects

You can then select those layers and:

  • ### Move them
  • ### Resize them
  • ### Hide them
  • ### Delete them
  • ### Change their prompt
  • ### Move them in front of or behind other layers

This feels much closer to editing an actual design instead of just telling an AI to regenerate something.

There is one pretty important warning, though.

There is currently no Undo button.

If you delete a layer by accident, it is gone. If you move something to the wrong position, you have to manually move it back.

So don't start randomly deleting everything like you're playing Photoshop after three beers.

How to Add New Objects With Layers

You aren't limited to the objects OurDream AI already found.

You can create completely new layers.

Let's say your character is sitting at a restaurant table and you forgot to add a bottle of champagne.

Instead of regenerating the entire page, click Generate New Layer, place the box where you want the object, and describe it.

For example:

Champagne bottle inside a silver ice bucket.

OurDream AI generates it as a new layer, and you can then move or resize it until it fits the scene.

This is really useful for adding small things that the original generator forgot.

Brush Editor vs Layers: Which Should You Use?

I would keep this simple.

Use the Brush Editor when one small part of the image is wrong.

For example:

  • ### Wrong hair
  • ### Bad hand
  • ### Wrong object
  • ### Small background problem
  • ### Incorrect text

Use Layers when you want more control over the actual composition.

For example:

  • ### Move a character
  • ### Resize something
  • ### Put an object behind another object
  • ### Remove an entire object
  • ### Add a new object
  • ### Change several parts of the page individually

Layers are more powerful, but they also cost more and have more limitations.

Creating Layers currently costs 80 DreamCoins, while a Brush Edit costs 20 DreamCoins.

Layers can also struggle with complicated pages. The guide found that pages with four or more panels may sometimes fail to create proper layers or miss smaller details.

So personally, I would use the Brush Editor for quick fixes and only open Layers when I actually need the extra control.

How Many Times Should You Edit an Image?

Don't keep editing the same image forever.

Every edit slightly reduces the quality of the image being sent back through the editor.

According to the beta testing:

  • ### Original image: Best quality
  • ### 1–2 edits: Usually works well
  • ### 3 edits: Still okay, but reliability starts dropping
  • ### 4+ edits: Can become unreliable

I would treat three edits as the maximum.

If you are already on edit number four and the AI still refuses to fix something, go back to an earlier version and try again from there.

Otherwise, you can end up spending more DreamCoins trying to save a broken page than it would have cost to simply regenerate it.

How to Turn an OurDream AI Comic Into a Video

One of the coolest parts of OurDream AI Comics is that your comic does not have to stay a comic.

You can take a finished comic page and turn it into a short AI video.

This is not a normal text-to-video generator where you write a prompt from scratch. OurDream AI uses your finished comic page as the starting image and animates what is already happening in the panels.

How Comic Videos Work

OurDream AI currently uses its Cinematic image-to-video model for comics.

At the time of writing, each video is:

  • ### 10 seconds long
  • ### Horizontal
  • ### Generated from your finished comic page
  • ### Able to use your speech bubbles as spoken dialogue
  • ### Controlled with optional Motion Notes

Basically, your comic page acts like a storyboard.

If your page has three panels, the video generator looks at those panels and tries to turn them into one short moving scene.

This is why I recommend getting your comic page completely right before turning it into a video. If the character, text, or background is already wrong in the comic, you are starting the video with the same problem.

How to Add Motion

When you generate the video, you can add Motion Notes.

This is where you tell OurDream AI how you want the characters and camera to move.

For example, you could tell it to slowly zoom toward a character, cut to another panel, pull the camera back, or add small movements like hair moving.

You don't have to write a huge prompt.

Just explain the movement you actually want.

How to Prompt Comic Motion

The easiest way to write Motion Notes is to go through the comic page in order.

For example:

Start on Panel 01 with a slow push toward Sarah. Cut to Panel 02 and hold on her face. Move to Panel 03 and slowly pull the camera back to show both characters.

That's already enough.

Focus mainly on:

Camera movement \+ character movement.

Don't rewrite the whole story again.

Your comic page already shows the story. The Motion Notes are there to tell the video generator how that story should move.

How Dialogue and Audio Work

This part is pretty clever.

OurDream AI reads the speech bubbles already on your comic page and turns them into spoken dialogue in the video.

So if Sarah's speech bubble says:

"Where have you been?"

the video generator can use that as Sarah's spoken line.

You do not need to put the dialogue inside your Motion Notes.

In fact, the beta guide found that dialogue written inside the Motion Notes is mostly ignored. The generator looks at the speech bubbles instead.

That means you should check your dialogue carefully before generating the video.

If the speech bubble has a typo, the video may literally say the typo.

How to Fix Incorrect Name Pronunciation

There is one funny problem with the audio system.

Because the video generator reads the text exactly as it appears in the speech bubble, it can pronounce some names incorrectly.

For example, the testing guide found that Ximena could be pronounced incorrectly when written normally. Changing the speech bubble to a phonetic spelling such as Himena helped the English pronunciation.

The same idea can be used for other names that the AI keeps pronouncing incorrectly.

Before generating your video, change the word inside the speech bubble to a spelling that produces the pronunciation you want.

The downside is obvious: that phonetic spelling is now visible inside the comic.

So I would only bother doing this when the pronunciation is noticeably bad.

OurDream AI Comics Limitations

OurDream AI Comics is impressive, but it is still a new feature and you will run into problems.

Most of them are not deal-breakers, but it helps to know about them before you start spending DreamCoins.

Desktop Only

Right now, you can only create comics on a desktop or laptop.

If you open Comic Studio on mobile, the editor tells you that you need a bigger screen and blocks access.

You can still read finished comics on mobile, but you cannot create or edit them there yet.

For me, this is not a huge problem because the editor has a lot of controls anyway, but it is definitely annoying if you mainly use your phone.

Character Consistency Problems

Model Sheets help keep your characters consistent, but they are not perfect.

The AI can still change small details between panels or confuse two characters if they look too similar.

This becomes more noticeable in busy scenes with several characters.

If two women have similar hair, outfits, or body shapes, the AI can sometimes mix them up.

The easiest way to reduce this is to make the characters visually different and clearly describe who is doing what in every panel.

Outfit and Pose Problems

This is probably one of the most common problems.

The AI does not always remember that a character is sitting, standing, lying down, or wearing a specific outfit.

For example:

Panel 01: Sarah is sitting on the bed.

Panel 02: Sarah talks to James.

If you don't say that Sarah is still sitting, the AI can suddenly make her stand up.

The same happens with clothes. A jacket you removed two panels ago can randomly come back.

The beta guide recommends repeating the character's current pose and outfit in every panel where it matters.

It feels repetitive, but it saves a lot of regenerations.

Dialogue and Text Problems

I would avoid generating dialogue directly inside the image whenever possible.

The image generator can:

  • ### Shorten your sentence
  • ### Rewrite it
  • ### Misspell words
  • ### Cut off part of the text

Long dialogue makes the problem even worse.

The separate speech bubble tool fixes most of this because you type the final dialogue yourself.

The AI can also randomly generate fake speech bubbles even if your prompt contains no dialogue.

Adding:

No text, no speech bubbles.

to the end of your prompt usually helps.

Brush Editor Limitations

The Brush Editor works well for small fixes, but it gets less reliable after you keep editing the same image.

According to the beta testing:

  • ### 1–2 edits usually work well
  • ### 3 edits are still okay
  • ### 4 or more edits can become unreliable

The editor can sometimes return basically the same image even though you clearly told it what to change.

If that happens, go back to an earlier version and try again instead of editing the already-edited image for the fifth time.

Also, if the brush itself is giving you bad results, the guide recommends trying a normal edit prompt without selecting an area.

Layer Limitations

Layers are powerful, but they are definitely not perfect yet.

The biggest problem is that complicated pages can fail to create proper layers.

This happens more often when the page has four or more panels or lots of objects.

Even when Layers work, they can miss smaller details.

There is also currently no Undo button.

If you delete a layer by accident, you can't simply press Ctrl+Z and bring it back. If you move something, you need to manually put it back.

Layers can also lose small background details like shadows.

So I would always check the result before deleting anything.

NSFW Generation Limitations

Yes, you can make NSFW comics, but explicit pages can be harder to generate than normal pages.

The beta guide found that Comic Studio can resist explicit prompts more often than OurDream AI's normal image generator.

That means you may need:

  • ### More regenerations
  • ### More edits
  • ### Slightly different wording

This also means NSFW comics can cost more DreamCoins because you are more likely to retry a page.

Another thing to watch is character age.

If the original profile image looks borderline young, the Model Sheet can also make the character look younger than you intended.

Make sure your characters clearly look like adults before generating explicit content.

Model Sheet Limitations

Model Sheets are created mainly from the character's profile image.

They do not reliably pull every detail from the character's written physical description.

This means things like:

  • ### Accessories
  • ### Weapons
  • ### Special gear
  • ### Small physical details

can be missing.

The guide recommends checking the Model Sheet before generating Page 1\. If something important is missing, fix it first.

Otherwise, you might generate ten pages and then realize your character has been missing an important item the entire time.

OurDream AI Comics Tips

After going through the Comic Studio guide, there are a few things I would definitely do every time.

Most of these tips are simple, but they can save you a lot of wasted DreamCoins.

Remove Page References When Changing Scenes

OurDream AI automatically uses the previous page as a reference.

This is great when the same scene continues.

But when you move somewhere completely different, remove it.

For example:

Page 1: Bar.

Page 2: Still at the bar.

Page 3: Hotel room.

Remove the bar reference before generating Page 3\.

Otherwise, the AI might start adding parts of the bar to your hotel room.

The same rule applies when you make a big change in outfit, time of day, location, or visual style.

Keep Dialogue Short

Comic dialogue should be short anyway, and OurDream AI gives you another reason to keep it that way.

Long dialogue is more likely to get shortened or changed when generated inside the image.

Even with the speech bubble tool, you have a maximum of 150 characters per bubble.

If a character has a lot to say, split it between panels.

It usually looks better and is easier to read.

Repeat Important Outfit and Pose Details

Never assume the AI remembers what happened one panel ago.

If Sarah is sitting, say she is sitting.

If she is wearing a red dress, say she is wearing a red dress.

If she took her jacket off, say she is not wearing the jacket anymore.

This is one of the easiest ways to stop your characters from randomly changing between panels.

Be Specific With Lighting and Backgrounds

Don't write:

Nice apartment with romantic lighting.

That gives the AI way too much freedom.

Write:

Small modern apartment, dark wooden floor, cream walls, warm amber light from two bedside lamps.

The guide found that literal descriptions work better than vague words like "cozy," "beautiful," or "modern."

The same goes for backgrounds.

If an important hallway, bedroom, bar, or apartment needs to stay the same, repeat the main details whenever they are visible.

Use Model Sheet Versions for Different Looks

You don't need to force one Model Sheet to do everything.

OurDream AI saves every Model Sheet version you generate.

You can switch between them later.

This is useful if your character has completely different looks during the story.

For example:

Normal version — casual clothes.

Date version — evening outfit.

Fantasy version — armor or special equipment.

If the difference is only a simple outfit change, you can usually just override it in the page prompt.

But for major appearance changes, having a separate Model Sheet can make things much easier.

Add Speech Bubbles After Generating

This is probably one of my biggest recommendations.

Generate the artwork first.

Make sure the characters, backgrounds, and poses look good.

Then add the dialogue afterward.

The separate speech bubble tool lets you type exactly what you want instead of hoping the image generator correctly spells an entire sentence.

It is easier, more reliable, and currently costs 0 DreamCoins to save the speech bubbles.

Don’t Over-Edit the Same Image

If an image needs one or two fixes, edit it.

If you are on edit number five and the page still looks fucked, stop.

Go back to the original generation or regenerate the page.

Repeated edits make the editor less reliable, and after around three edits the quality starts dropping.

You can very quickly waste more DreamCoins trying to save one bad generation than it would cost to generate a new one.

Check Your Character Before Generating Page 1

Do this before you start.

Seriously.

Look at the Model Sheet and check:

  • ### Does the character actually look right?
  • ### Is the age clearly adult?
  • ### Is the hair correct?
  • ### Are important accessories there?
  • ### Is important gear missing?
  • ### Is this the version you want to use?

The Model Sheet gets used as the visual reference throughout the comic.

If it is wrong on Page 1, there is a good chance you will keep fighting the same problem for the rest of the story.

Fixing the Model Sheet first is much cheaper than fixing ten comic pages later.

How to Publish an OurDream AI Comic

Once your comic is finished, you can publish it so other people can read it.

Before you hit publish, I would spend a few minutes checking everything. It is much easier to fix a bad page now than notice it after the comic is already public.

Add Your Comic Name and Description

Your comic name and description are added through Edit Comic.

These are separate from the title that appears on your Cover Page. Your comic name and description are what people see when your comic appears in the public Comics section.

I would keep the description simple.

Something like:

Sarah meets a stranger at a bar and decides to go home with him. What starts as a one-night stand quickly turns into something more.

You don't need to explain the entire story.

The guide recommends:

  • Introduce the main character
  • Explain the basic situation
  • Give people the main conflict
  • Don't spoil everything

Basically, give people enough information to make them want to click.

Add Tags

You can also add tags to your comic.

These help people understand what the comic is about and can make it easier to discover.

I would use tags that actually describe the story.

If you made an anime romance comic, use tags around that.

If you made an adult fantasy comic, use tags that match that.

Don't just add random popular tags hoping to get more views. That normally just makes your listing look spammy.

Organize Your Pages

You can change the order of your pages with the Organize button.

So if Page 6 accidentally needs to be before Page 5, you don't need to regenerate anything. Just move the pages around.

Before publishing, I would quickly click through the entire comic from beginning to end.

Make sure the story actually flows correctly.

This sounds obvious, but when you have been generating and editing pages for an hour, it is surprisingly easy to put something in the wrong order.

Check Your Comic Before Publishing

Before publishing, I would check:

  • Does the cover look good?
  • Is the title easy to read?
  • Are the pages in the right order?
  • Do the characters look consistent?
  • Are the right Model Sheets being used?
  • Is all dialogue spelled correctly?
  • Does every speech bubble point to the right character?
  • Did you add a comic name and description?
  • Did you add useful tags?
  • Does anything look obviously broken?

The beta guide recommends checking these things before sending your comic for public review.

Remember that public comics can still go through human moderation, so getting a page through the AI generator does not automatically mean the final comic will be approved.

I think OurDream AI Comics is definitely worth trying if you want to create your own AI comic, especially if you have no drawing experience.

You can create your own characters, keep them across multiple pages, choose different comic styles, add proper speech bubbles, edit individual parts of the image, and even turn finished pages into videos.

That is a lot more advanced than simply generating five random AI images and calling it a comic.

and go make coffee.

You still need to guide the AI page by page.
