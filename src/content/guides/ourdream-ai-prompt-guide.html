Knowing some basic OurDream AI prompt engineering is key to making the most out of your OurDream AI subscription. It will not only save you a ton of tokens but also open a door with a ton of creative opportunities like generating hentai using the OurDream AI comic generator.

This is a zero-BS OurDream AI prompt guide for beginners. You’ll learn the types of prompts, how to use them, and where to use them.

How Prompting Works in OurDream AI

It doesn’t matter whether you are generating an AI character, generating a video, or an in-chat image — all prompts work the same.

  1. You tell OurDream what you want

  2. The AI reads your prompt

  3. It creates the result based on your instructions

  4. If the result is wrong, you change the prompt and try again

If you type a prompt and don’t get what you want, then 9 out of 10 times it is because the prompt is too vague or unnecessarily complicated. OurDream AI specifically says that long complicated prompts are not automatically better.

OurDream’s own help docs make the same point: prompt quality is about clarity, not stuffing in extra keywords.¹

Key takeaway

Prompt quality isn’t about stuffing in more keywords. It’s about giving the right information to the right generator.

And that last part matters, because an image prompt, video prompt, and character prompt need very different information.

The 4 Types of OurDream AI Prompts

OurDream AI has four places where prompts can be used: in the character generator, when generating images, when generating videos, and during roleplay.

Each one behaves slightly differently.

Character Prompts

You use character prompts when creating a character. These prompts describe things that are part of the character, such as their face, body type, eyes, hair, and other physical features.

Important

The important thing to remember is that character prompts should describe permanent features, not temporary ones.

ATemporary detail

For example, if you add “wearing a hospital uniform” to the character prompt, OurDream may keep trying to generate that outfit later, even when you want your character to wear something different.

BPermanent feature

Something like “big blue eyes” makes much more sense because that is a feature you normally want the character to keep.

Image Prompts

Image prompts are used to tell OurDream what you want to see in an image.

A simple structure you can use is:

Image prompt structure

subject action/pose environment clothing/props lighting composition visual style

Put the most important details first. This makes it easier for the image generator to understand what the image should focus on.

Using a structure like this also helps you include enough detail without making the prompt messy or unnecessarily long.

NO STRUCTURE

A woman at the beach at sunset wearing a black dress, cinematic, sitting down, beautiful lighting, realistic photo.

WITH STRUCTURE

Woman → sitting on the sand → tropical beach at sunset → black summer dress → warm golden-hour lighting → medium shot, eye level → realistic photography

The second version gives the same basic idea, but each part of the prompt has a clear purpose.

Video Prompts

Video prompts describe what happens over time. An image prompt is like describing a photo, while a video prompt is more like giving directions for a short scene — what the character does, how they move, and what the camera does.

The template I am always using is:

Video prompt template

starting state subject movement expression/action camera movement pacing ending state

Tip

When generating videos on OurDream AI you can actually pick from text-to-video or image-to-video. The second option is recommended, as it’s significantly easier to get what you want.

Chat & Roleplay Prompts

Besides normal chatting and roleplaying, you can also use OOC commands.

OOC stands for “out of character.” These are short instructions you give the AI to control how the chat or roleplay should continue.

You can use them to do things like:

  • make replies shorter or longer
  • change the tone of the conversation
  • move the story forward
  • tell the AI to focus on a specific detail
  • correct something the AI misunderstood

For example:

OOC command 
(OOC: Keep your replies short and playful.)

The AI then uses that instruction to guide its next replies without making it part of the actual roleplay.

OurDream AI gives you plenty of chat customization options like reply length and writing style, so these commands are rarely needed. That said, they can be helpful sometimes.

Normal Prompts vs JSON Prompts in OurDream AI

JSON prompts are structured prompts used for AI image generation. At first glance, they may look complicated, but they are actually very easy to use. They let you describe an image in much more detail than a regular prompt, which can often lead to a more accurate result.

They are especially useful when you want to copy a pose, style, composition, or even the overall look of another image using your own character.

For example, let’s say you find a great image on Pinterest and want to recreate it in OurDream AI with your own character. A JSON prompt lets you break the image down into smaller details, such as the pose, hand placement, camera angle, clothing, lighting, and background. This gives the AI much clearer instructions about what you want.

Does Prompt Structure Actually Matter?

For simple images, not always.

I tested the same idea with prompts that became more and more detailed. Surprisingly, OurDream got the main idea right every time.

That makes sense. If you ask for something simple like “woman drinking coffee in Paris,” there are only a few important things the AI needs to understand.

Prompt structure becomes much more useful when you want specific details to be correct.

Common OurDream AI Prompting Mistakes

Even a good AI model can give you a bad result if your prompt is confusing. These are some of the mistakes I see most often.

Making Your Prompt Too Vague

The AI cannot know what you have in your head.

If you simply write:

Too vague 
woman at the beach

OurDream has to decide almost everything for you, including the pose, outfit, lighting, camera angle, and what the beach looks like.

If you care about a detail, include it in your prompt. You do not need to describe everything, only the things you actually want control over.

Adding Too Many Instructions

More details do not always mean a better prompt.

If you give OurDream too many instructions at once, it has more things to understand and more things it can get wrong.

Tip

Start with the important details first. You can always add more if the first result is missing something.

Putting Temporary Details in a Character Prompt

Character prompts should mainly describe things you want your character to keep.

For example, blue eyes or long blonde hair make sense in a character prompt.

Something like wearing a hospital uniform does not. You may want her wearing something completely different in your next image or roleplay.

Keep temporary things like outfits, locations, and situations out of the permanent character description when possible.

Describing an Image When You Are Generating a Video

A video prompt should explain what happens, not only what the scene looks like.

For example:

This mostly describes an image.

Instead, tell the video generator what should move:

IMAGE PROMPT

blonde woman on a beach, black dress, sunset

VIDEO PROMPT

She slowly walks toward the camera while her dress moves in the wind. She then looks over her shoulder as the camera follows her.

Think of an image prompt as describing a photo and a video prompt as giving directions for a short scene.

Changing Too Many Things at Once

If a generation is almost right, do not rewrite the entire prompt.

Let’s say you like the image, but her hand is in the wrong place. Change the instruction about her hand and generate again.

If you change the pose, outfit, camera angle, lighting, and location at the same time, you will not know which change actually helped.

Using the Same Prompt Style for Every Generator

What I learnedHerman Carter · Lead Reviewer

One of the biggest things I learned while testing OurDream is that different image generators can respond differently to the exact same prompt style.

AVivid 1 and Vivid 2

For example, I tested JSON prompts with Vivid 1 and Vivid 2, and both followed them surprisingly well. I could give very detailed instructions and still get an accurate result.

BDreamy

I then tried similar JSON prompts with the default Dreamy generator. The results were much less accurate and the image quality was worse. Dreamy actually worked better for me when I turned the same instructions into a normal paragraph prompt.

So if a prompt works badly, it does not always mean the prompt itself is bad. Try changing how you write it or test it with another generator.

Key takeaway

There is no single prompt format that works best with every OurDream model.

My OurDream AI Prompting Workflow

It doesn’t matter if I am creating an image prompt, video prompt, or character prompt. My workflow almost always starts outside of OurDream AI in ChatGPT.

It may sound obvious, but AI is really good at creating prompts for other AI tools.

  1. I Explain What I Want to ChatGPT

    I normally open a ChatGPT conversation and explain what I want using the voice button.

    I prefer talking instead of typing because I naturally give a lot more detail. When I type, I tend to keep things short. When I talk, I explain the idea, what I want it to look like, what is important, and what I don’t want.

    You don’t need to make it sound professional. Just explain the idea like you would explain it to another person.

  2. I Ask ChatGPT to Turn It Into a Prompt

    Once ChatGPT understands what I want, I ask it to turn everything I said into a prompt for OurDream AI.

    The prompt will look different depending on what I am creating. An image prompt should describe what you want to see, while a video prompt should focus much more on what happens and how things move.

    If I am using a specific OurDream generator, I also tell ChatGPT which one I am using.

  3. I Add References When I Have Them

    If I am trying to recreate a certain image, pose, style, or scene, I also upload the reference to ChatGPT.

    This is especially useful when creating detailed image or video prompts because ChatGPT can help break down things that are easy to miss, like the camera angle, hand position, lighting, background, and composition.

  4. I Test the Prompt in OurDream

    Then I paste the prompt into OurDream and generate.

    I rarely expect the first generation to be perfect. I first look at what it got right and what it got wrong.

  5. I Fix Only What Went Wrong

    If the result is almost right, I don’t start over.

    For example, if everything looks good but the pose is wrong, I go back to ChatGPT and explain exactly what was wrong with the pose.

    Then I update that part of the prompt and try again.

    This makes it much easier to understand what is actually improving the result.

  6. I Save Prompts That Work

    When I find a prompt structure that works well, I save it.

    After a while, you start building your own templates for different things like images, videos, characters, and certain OurDream generators.

    You don’t need to reinvent the prompt every single time.

OurDream AI Prompting FAQ

Do Longer Prompts Give Better Results?

Not always.

A longer prompt gives the AI more information, but it also gives it more things to get wrong.

Use enough detail to explain what you want. Don’t add details just to make the prompt longer.

Should I Use JSON or Normal Prompts?

It depends on the generator.

In my testing, Vivid 1 and Vivid 2 worked very well with detailed JSON prompts. The default Dreamy generator was much less accurate with them and gave me better results with normal paragraph prompts.

So I wouldn’t say JSON is always better. Test both and see what works best with the generator you are using.

Why Is OurDream Not Following My Prompt?

There can be a few reasons.

Your prompt may be too vague, you may be asking for too many things at once, or some of your instructions may conflict with each other.

It can also simply be the generator you are using. If the prompt looks good but the result keeps failing, try another generator or rewrite the same instructions in a different format.

Should I Use the Same Prompt for Every OurDream Generator?

No.

Different generators can respond differently to the same prompt.

A prompt that works perfectly with Vivid 2 may not work as well with Dreamy. That is why I always test the prompt with the generator I actually plan to use.

What Should I Do If My Image Is Almost Perfect?

Change only the part that is wrong.

If the outfit, background, and lighting are good but the hand position is wrong, only change the hand instruction.

If you rewrite everything, you may fix the hand but accidentally ruin everything else.

Should I Put Outfits and Locations in My Character Prompt?

Usually not if they are temporary.

Things like eye color, hair, or other permanent features make sense in a character prompt.

An outfit, location, or situation can change, so those details are usually better added when creating the image or starting the scene.

Do I Need to Know Prompt Engineering to Use OurDream AI?

No.

You can get good results with very simple prompts.

Prompting becomes more useful when you want more control over small details. You can also use ChatGPT to help turn a simple idea into a more detailed prompt.

What Is the Biggest Prompting Mistake Beginners Make?

Trying to make the perfect prompt before generating anything.

Start with what you know you want, generate, look at the result, and then improve it.

It is usually much faster than spending ten minutes trying to write one massive prompt before you even know what the AI will get wrong.
